Synthetic criteria and preferences

The option matrix comes from the three computed arms. Higher integrated producer and consumer indices are chosen benefits for this exercise. Lower cumulative exposure and shorter time under a changed policy are chosen costs. These preferences can compete.

AHP pairwise priorities

A ratio of 1 gives equal priority. A value above 1 favors the first criterion. A value below 1 favors the second. Enter a decimal between 1/9 and 9.

Normalization and calculated weights

Min–max scaling puts benefits and reversed costs on [0, 1], with higher values preferred. A benefit uses (x − min)/(max − min), a cost uses (max − x)/(max − min), and a constant column becomes zero. The hybrid is the equal arithmetic mean of AHP, EWM and CRITIC.

AHP, EWM and CRITIC calculations

AHP normalizes row geometric means. The consistency ratio uses the mean of (Aw)ᵢ/wᵢ to estimate λmax and compares the resulting index with a random index. A value at or above 0.10 flags judgments to review.

EWM uses pᵢ = zᵢ/Σz and normalizes information 1 − entropy. It uses 0 log 0 = 0. Constant columns contribute zero information. When all information is zero, the weights are uniform.

CRITIC multiplies each standard deviation by summed Pearson conflict (1 − correlation). Constant columns are excluded. If no conflict remains, it uses relative spread, or uniform weights if every column is constant.

TOPSIS and preference change

TOPSIS measures distance to the best and worst weighted oriented values. Closeness is distance from worst divided by the sum of both distances. Identical scores share a rank. The second preference doubles the cumulative exposure weight before renormalizing.
